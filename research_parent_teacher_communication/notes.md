## Key Papers Identified

### 1. Communicating Digitally: Building Preschool Teacher-Parent Partnerships Via Digital Technologies During COVID-19
**Authors:** Jennifer J. Chen, Dahana E. Rivera-Vernazza
**Year:** 2022
**Source:** Early Childhood Education Journal (PMC9244098)
**Key Findings:**
- Investigated digital communication (email, text, ClassDojo) between preschool teachers and parents.
- **Positive outcomes:** Enhanced school readiness (Barnett et al., 2020) and better academic performance (Galindo & Sheldon, 2012).
- **Digital benefits:** Bidirectional, personalized digital communication optimizes student outcomes like academic engagement and attendance (See et al., 2020).
- **Parental Involvement:** Digital platforms like ClassDojo promote "proactive parent involvement" (home-based support).

### 2. Influences of Parent Engagement in Early Childhood Education Centers and the Home on Kindergarten School Readiness
**Authors:** Barnett MA, Paschall KW, Mastergeorge AM, Cutshaw CA, Warren SM
**Year:** 2020
**Source:** Early Childhood Research Quarterly, 53, 260-273.
**Key Findings (from Abstract):**
- **Mediation Model:** ECE parent engagement practices (e.g., sending info home) are linked to greater parent engagement at home.
- **Impact:** ECE practices were *indirectly* associated with kindergarten academic readiness through increases in home learning activities.
- **Equity:** Connections were strongest for families with low household incomes.
- **Takeaway:** Apps that facilitate "sending home info" drive home engagement, which drives readiness.

### 3. A systematic review of the impact of technology-mediated parental engagement on student outcomes
**Authors:** See BH, Gorard S, El-Soufi N, Lu B, Siddiqui N, Dong L
**Year:** 2020
**Source:** Educational Research and Evaluation
**Key Findings (from Secondary Summary):**
- **Bi-directional is key:** Bi-directional communication (home-to-school and school-to-home) had a significant impact on social-behavioral competence.
- **One-way fails:** One-way communication alone did not significantly relate to positive effects on academic or social-emotional functioning.
- **Collaboration:** Collaboration (planning/problem-solving) impacted academic achievement and mental health.

### 4. The underutilized potential of teacher-to-parent communication: Evidence from a field experiment
**Authors:** Kraft MA, Rogers T
**Year:** 2015
**Source:** Economics of Education Review
**Key Findings (from Boston Fed summary & Abstract):**
- **Intervention:** Weekly one-sentence individualized messages from teachers to parents.
- **Result:** Decreased course failure by 41% (from 15.8% to 9.3%).
- **Mechanism:** Prevented dropouts; shaped parent-child conversations (emphasizing improvement vs. just "doing well").
- **Cost-effective:** Cost per additional credit earned was <1/10th of typical district cost.
- **Takeaway:** Frequent, short, individualized digital messages have a massive impact on retention and success.

### 5. Predictors of parents' satisfaction with their children's school
**Authors:** Friedman, B.A., Bobrowski, P.E., and D. Markow
**Year:** 2007
**Source:** Journal of Educational Administration
**Key Findings (from ResearchGate snippet):**
- Identified five sources of parental satisfaction:
    1. Receiving information from the school about their children.
    2. The degree of involvement they were afforded.
    3. School resources.
    4. Leadership effectiveness.
    5. Budget management.
- **Fantuzzo et al. (2006)** identified "teacher contact" (communication) as one of the three most important dimensions of parent satisfaction.

### 6. School and home connections and children’s kindergarten achievement gains: The mediating role of family involvement
**Authors:** Galindo C, Sheldon SB
**Year:** 2012
**Source:** Early Childhood Research Quarterly
**Context:** Cited as evidence that school-home connections improve achievement via the mechanism of family involvement.

## Themes to Develop
1.  **Academic & Developmental Outcomes:**
    *   Direct link: Bi-directional communication -> Social-behavioral competence (See et al.).
    *   Indirect link: ECE engagement practices -> Home learning -> School Readiness (Barnett et al.).
    *   Failure reduction: 41% reduction in failure rates via weekly messages (Kraft & Rogers).
2.  **Parental Engagement:**
    *   Communication is the *driver* of engagement.
    *   "Proactive involvement" (home-based) is enabled by digital tools (Chen 2022).
3.  **Digital Efficacy:**
    *   Bi-directional > One-way.
    *   Apps/Digital tools bridge the gap when face-to-face is hard (COVID/Busy schedules).
    *   "Nudges" (short messages) are highly effective (Kraft & Rogers).
4.  **School Quality/Marketing:**
    *   Parent satisfaction is directly linked to "receiving information" and "teacher contact" (Friedman et al., Fantuzzo et al.).
    *   Satisfied parents drive retention and school choice.
